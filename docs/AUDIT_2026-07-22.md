# Full-pipeline verification and audit — 2026-07-22

Everything below was produced by executing the pipeline on this machine (Windows 11, Python 3.12.10,
torch 2.13.0+**cpu** — no CUDA, sdv 1.37.3, scikit-learn 1.9.0, pandas 2.3.3, numpy 2.5.1) from the
raw NHANES `.xpt` files, and by reading the source. No number here is quoted from a pre-existing
result file without being recomputed or re-derived. Where a hypothesis was tested and **failed**, it
is recorded as failed.

Claim status lives in [`reference.md`](../reference.md) §1; conventions and the frozen table live in
`CLAUDE.md`. This document is a point-in-time audit, not a third source of truth.

## 3. Bugs and issues

### 3.1 SDV baselines sampled under a fixed internal seed — **fixed**

`gc_synthetic_seed4{2..6}.csv` had **one MD5 across all five seeds**. `generate_sdv` called
`set_all_seeds(seed)` and then `cls.load(ckpt).sample(...)`, but SDV wraps sampling in its own
`FIXED_RNG_SEED = 73251` and ignores the global RNG. GaussianCopula's fit is deterministic, so
identical model + identical sampler seed = identical rows.

Consequence: GaussianCopula's published `1.661 ± 0.000` was **a single sample presented as a 5-seed
mean**, and the non-zero ±std on its utility and MIA columns was evaluator noise, not generator
variance. TVAE was affected the same way but less visibly — it varied only through its per-seed
weights, never through sampling.

Fixed in [baselines/sdv_baselines.py](../baselines/sdv_baselines.py) with
`synthesizer._set_random_state(seed)`. The private name is deliberate: `set_random_state` does not
exist on SDV 1.37.3. Effect on the reported standard deviations:

| | fidelity std | dcr_ratio std |
|---|---|---|
| gc | 0.00000 → **0.00120** | 0.00000 → **0.00510** |
| tvae | 0.00674 → 0.00636 | 0.02308 → 0.02136 |
| vae / ctgan | unchanged | unchanged |

The finding survives the fix — GaussianCopula still has by far the best DCR ratio (1.676) on mediocre
fidelity — but it now has honest error bars. Axis pass counts (paper Table 2) did not change.

### 3.2 Figure 3 label collisions — **fixed**

The "DCR passes, MIA fires" annotation sat on top of the DOMIAS markers and the legend overlapped the
DOMIAS curve. Confirmed by rendering and viewing the PNG, and re-verified after the fix.

### 3.3 `PHQ9_TOTAL` is a column that never exists

The project's central data-integrity story is built around dropping `PHQ9_TOTAL`. But
`build_dataset` loads only `SEQN` plus the nine mapped DPQ items from `P_DPQ.xpt` and derives
`PHQ9_RAW` itself — **no `PHQ9_TOTAL` column is ever constructed**. So its entry in `LEAKAGE_COLS`
drops nothing, and `assert "PHQ9_TOTAL" not in df.columns` at
[preprocess.py:219](../preprocess.py#L219) **cannot fail**. The real guards are the nine DPQ-item
asserts on the following lines, which do work.

Not a live leak — the leakage fix is genuinely in force — but the narrative in `CLAUDE.md`,
`reference.md` §2, `paper/leakage_disclosure.md` and paper §III-C all name the wrong column, and one
of the two headline asserts is decorative.

### 3.4 The `0.9999` in paper Table 7 cannot be regenerated

Nothing in the repository computes the pre-fix TRTR AUC. `build_dataset(drop_leakage=False)` retains
the DPQ items but still drops `PHQ9_RAW`, and no script fits a classifier on the leaky feature set.
[paper/leakage_disclosure.md](../paper/leakage_disclosure.md) states the number with no method, seed,
or code path. The `0.7125` half **is** independently confirmed — it is `trtr_auc` in
`faa_*_seed42.csv`. A ~15-line script would close this.

### 3.5 CTGAN does no conditional resampling and no mode-specific normalisation

[models/ctgan/train.py:87](../models/ctgan/train.py#L87) takes the condition vector directly from each
real batch's labels. With 75.7% of training rows in class 0, the generator receives almost no
gradient for classes 3–4 (2.9% combined). Both of the mechanisms that distinguish CTGAN from a
conditional GAN in Xu et al. 2019 — training-by-sampling and mode-specific normalisation — are
absent. The model is a conditional WGAN-GP. The paper's §IV-A *description* is accurate, but the
model is named CTGAN throughout and Xu et al. is cited for the architecture.

### 3.6 VAE has three collapsed columns

| column | real mean | VAE synthetic mean | per-column fidelity |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Total_Sugar` | 0.112 | 0.0004 | **0.007** |
| `Total_Caffeine` | 0.031 | 0.000 (3 distinct values) | 0.148 |
| `Avg_Drinks_Per_Day` | 0.096 | 0.0003 | 0.237 |

Mechanism: MSE reconstruction (`reduction="mean"`) on MinMax-scaled columns with extreme right tails
drives the decoder to the conditional mean, which for these columns is ≈ 0. The VAE row in Table 1 is
produced by a generator with 3 of 10 continuous columns dead, and that is most of why its fidelity
(0.753) is the worst in the matrix.

### 3.7 `clinical_signal_check` cannot fail

It flags a synthetic/real Spearman divergence > 0.15 on three pairs. In the real training data those
correlations are −0.013 (Age), −0.031 (Sleep_Hours_Weekday) and +0.063 (Avg_Drinks_Per_Day) — all
effectively zero. A synthetic dataset with *no* association on any of them would still pass.
`clinical_flags = 0` in all 20 runs is therefore uninformative, and the paper lists the check as a
reported quantity.

### 3.8 Preprocessing statistics are fit before splitting

`MinMaxScaler`, median imputation and mode imputation are all fit on the full 8,965 rows at
[preprocess.py:216](../preprocess.py#L216), and the split happens at line 243. Every downstream number
lives in a scaled space defined partly by test rows. The effect on this audit is small — all metrics
compare distributions within the same space — but it is a reviewable defect and trivially avoidable.

Smaller items in the same file: DPQ sentinels 7/9 ("don't know"/"refused") map to 0, which biases
`Depression_Severity` toward class 0; `Race` and `Race_Ext` are near-redundant encodings of the same
variable, both counted toward the "23 features"; `val_frac` at line 247 is computed and never used.

### 3.9 A hypothesis that was tested and rejected

Five of the ten "continuous" columns are actually discrete (`Sleep_Hours_Weekday` 24 levels,
`Drinking_Freq` 11, `Avg_Drinks_Per_Day` 14, `Binge_Drinking_Freq` 11, `Age` 63), and unlike the
categorical columns they are never snapped. Essentially **0%** of synthetic values land on a valid
real level. I expected this to inflate `dcr_ratio` — every synthetic row sitting off-lattice while
the holdout sits on it.

Measured directly: snapping those columns moves ctgan 1.0132 → 1.0171 and gc 1.6611 → 1.6651.
**Negligible. Axis 3 is not biased by this**, and the claim does not go in the paper. It remains a
data-*validity* issue: a released record with `Drinking_Freq = 3.72` is not a valid response to a
coded NHANES item.

### 3.10 Documentation drift

- `reference.md` §4 lists "Synthetic data stays in [0,1]" as enforced by `check_consistency.py`. It
  is not — that script checks fidelity, DCR, categorical levels, label integrality, row copies and
  attack controls, but never range. (The invariant does hold: verified min 0.0000 / max 1.0000 across
  all seed-42 synthetic files.)
- `docs/section 2/06_PAPER_REFRAME.md` contribution 1 says GaussianCopula has "the worst fidelity
  (~0.71)". 0.717 was gc's *utility* ratio; its fidelity is 0.834 and the worst fidelity is the VAE's
  0.753. The paper draft states it correctly.
- `data/processed/splits.pkl` still exists on disk although `reference.md` records its removal and
  `preprocess.py` no longer writes it.
- `experiments/attribute_inference_dpq.py` and `experiments/aia_results.csv` are an entire unreported
  experiment. Its result is a wide-variance null (synthetic-trained attack AUC 0.46–0.64 against a
  real-data bound of 0.66–0.71, with negative "recovered fraction" in 2 of 15 runs), it predates the
  `n = |train|` freeze, and it is not covered by `check_consistency.py`. It does **not** support
  "leakage rises with fidelity" — gc at fidelity 0.88 spans 0.46–0.64, the VAE at 0.70 spans
  0.52–0.62.

| VAE row of Table 1 | paper §VI-A | Correct as measured, but it is the output of a generator with three dead columns (§3.6). |

**Axis 2 hides a total failure on the clinically important classes.** The gate is a macro-averaged
OvR AUC ratio, and CTGAN clears it comfortably at 0.935. Per class, seed 42 (from
`utility_per_class_*.csv`, which the audit writes but the paper never reports):

| model | TSTR AUC class 3 | TSTR AUC class 4 | TSTR **F1** class 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| vae | 0.512 | 0.500 | **0.000** |
| ctgan | 0.738 | 0.566 | **0.000** |
| tvae | 0.661 | 0.680 | **0.000** |
| gc | 0.534 | **0.391** | **0.000** |
| *real data (TRTR)* | *0.697* | *0.765* | — |

No synthetic-trained classifier makes a single correct positive prediction for severe depression, and
GaussianCopula ranks severe cases *below* chance (0.391). A reader of Table 1 would conclude CTGAN's
synthetic data is 93.5% as useful as real data; on the class that matters clinically it is useless.
The paper states in §III-B that per-class results are reported "as well as macro-averaged" — they are
computed, but §VI reports only the macro ratio.

**One model in the main matrix does show a nearest-neighbour signal.** TVAE's `dcr_share_close` is
0.103 — 10% of its rows sit closer to the training set than the holdout 5th percentile, against 1.6%
(ctgan), 0.15% (vae) and 0.01% (gc) — and it is the only model failing Axis 3 (0.798). Its DOMIAS AUC
is nevertheless 0.523, a pass. This is the dial's finding in mirror image: here Axis 3 fires and
Axis 4 does not. Worth a sentence in §VI-A; currently unremarked.

## 5. Suggestions for improvement

Root-caused, in descending order of value.

**1. Axis 1's binding constraint is preprocessing, not the gate and not the architectures.** The
paper attributes universal Axis-1 failure to a demanding gate on survey data. The per-column evidence
says otherwise — every model fails on the same handful of columns, and they are exactly the
heavy-tailed, zero-inflated ones:

| column | skew | median | worst-model fidelity |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Total_Caffeine` | 7.85 | 0.020 | 0.148 |
| `Avg_Drinks_Per_Day` | 3.68 | 0.071 | 0.214 |
| `Total_Sugar` | 2.57 | 0.096 | 0.007 |
| `Binge_Drinking_Freq` | 1.40 | **0.000** | 0.270 |

MinMax scaling puts 99% of the caffeine mass in the bottom 18% of the range. A quantile or log1p
transform before scaling — or explicit zero-inflation handling — is a preprocessing change that
plausibly moves every model's fidelity at once, and it would test the paper's own claim that the gate
is at fault. Cheap to run; the checkpoints would need regenerating.

**2. Report per-class utility in §VI.** The data already exists in every run. The macro ratio alone
overstates utility in a way a clinical reviewer will catch immediately.

**3. Close the matched-model-class objection.** This remains the paper's largest unanswered
criticism, and this audit did not change it: DOMIAS estimates by KDE and the dial *is* a KDE, and the
RF cross-family check has no sensitivity (0.513–0.537 at *every* bandwidth including total
memorisation). An overfit neural dial is the direct answer.

**4. Fix or delete `clinical_signal_check`.** Either calibrate the threshold against the actual
effect sizes in this dataset, or remove it. A check that cannot fail is worse than no check — it
looks like coverage.

**5. Fit preprocessing statistics on train only.** Scaler and imputation, fit on `train`, applied to
`val`/`test`.

**6. Add the pre-fix TRTR script** so paper Table 7 is reproducible rather than asserted.

**7. Rename CTGAN or implement training-by-sampling.** "Conditional WGAN-GP" is accurate and costs
nothing; implementing the real thing would likely help the rare classes in point 2.

**8. Artifact availability.** `.gitignore` excludes `paper/`, `evaluation/results/`,
`data/processed/`, `models/saved/` **and `CLAUDE.md`**. The paper draft and every number in it are
untracked, and nothing from this work is committed. A fresh clone reproduces nothing. Un-ignoring
`paper/paper.md`, `paper/figures/*`, and the small summary CSVs (`faa_*.csv`, `leakage_dial.csv`,
`mia_power.csv`, `attack_validation.csv`) is a two-line change and is a submission requirement at any
venue with an artifact track.

Lower value: `Decoder.forward` loops over 24 columns in Python every forward pass (vectorise with a
boolean mask); `config.VAE_KL_MAX_WEIGHT = 1.0` is documented as "full KL" but both loss terms use
`mean`, so the KL is effectively down-weighted by latent_dim; drop `Race` or `Race_Ext`; delete
`data/processed/splits.pkl` and `val_frac`.

## 6. Research-level assessment

**Classification: publishable research — as a methodology / negative-results contribution, at a
venue that takes them.** ML4H Findings, as `06_PAPER_REFRAME.md` targets, is the right level. It is
*not* a main-track novel-method paper, and the project's own documents already say so.

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Novelty** | Moderate, and correctly bounded. The bare claim "DCR is uninformative about MIA risk" is **scooped** by Yao et al. 2025 (*The DCR Delusion*), which the project identified itself and positions against in `reference.md` §3. The surviving delta is real but narrow: clinical survey domain, the fidelity mechanism *measured* rather than argued, and a located gate-flip operating point. `dcr_ratio` is Platzer & Reutterer's metric; DOMIAS is used unmodified; the four-axis taxonomy is explicitly an instantiation of existing checklists, not a new framework. |
| **Technical complexity** | Moderate. Two from-scratch generators (a VAE and a WGAN-GP), two library baselines, four evaluation axes, a validated attack, a bandwidth sweep. No single component is hard; the integration and the calibration discipline are the work. |
| **Methodology** | **The strongest dimension.** Holdout calibration on every axis, effect sizes instead of p-values with the reason stated, an attack gated by positive *and* negative controls, a consistency checker that recomputes metrics from disk, and a documented retraction of the project's own prior headline. The negative control is the piece most applied work omits, and it is what caught the original error. |
| **Innovation** | Low-to-moderate. The leakage dial is the genuinely clever move — turning "we have no run where anything leaks" into a one-parameter family that sweeps through the gate. Everything else is careful assembly of existing parts. |
| **Experimental design** | Good, with one real hole. 4 × 5 matrix, 7 × 5 dial, a release-size power sweep that excludes the confound the null depended on, and an ablation disclosed as metric-gaming rather than reported as a result. The hole: the dial and the attack share a model class, and the intended cross-family check **failed** and is documented as failing. |
| **Validation** | Strong. Attack controls 0.929 / 0.503. Power null flat across 12.5×. `check_consistency.py` green over 59 files. Splits byte-reproducible from raw data. VAE/CTGAN bit-exact across a full re-run. |
| **Practical significance** | Genuine and narrow. §VII-D's five practitioner rules are actionable and are the kind of thing applied health-synthesis papers routinely get wrong. The public-data caveat is stated: NHANES is already public, so the privacy stakes are simulated. |
| **Reproducibility** | **Mixed — verified strong in substance, weak in packaging.** Determinism is confirmed by direct re-execution, and the guards are real. But nothing is committed, the paper and all results are gitignored, and one headline number (0.9999) has no code path. |
| **Comparison with prior work** | Honest and unusually well-mapped in `reference.md` §3 — including the entry that names the closest competitor as scooping the bare claim. But **§II Related Work is an unwritten stub with zero verified DOIs**. Until a human writes it, this is not a submittable paper. |

**What lifts it above a Master's-level project** is the epistemic discipline: the project found its
own headline finding to be a measurement artifact, retracted it in writing, kept the broken artifact
as the cautionary tale, and rebuilt the evidence base with controls. The `reference.md` claim ledger
distinguishes ACTIVE / RETRACTED / **NOT SUPPORTED**, and the RF cross-family check that failed is
recorded as failed rather than quietly dropped. That is research practice, not coursework.

**What holds it back from a stronger classification**: the central claim's novelty is bounded by a
2025 paper doing the same thing more broadly; one attack family, with the second one attempted and
found non-functional; the matched-KDE objection unanswered; the cross-model correlation not
significant (p = 0.115); the utility axis overstating usefulness on the clinically important classes
(§4); and no formal-privacy (DP) comparator.
